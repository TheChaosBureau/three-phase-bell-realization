# The Torus of Joint Angles: A Geometric Picture of Entanglement

## 1. What the “Torus” Is

In an entangled two-particle system (e.g. two photons or two spins), each particle has an internal orientation degree of freedom:

- Alice’s particle: an angle  θ_A  
- Bob’s particle: an angle  θ_B  

Each angle is periodic:  
θ ≡ θ + 2π  

So the space of possibilities is:

- A circle for Alice (S¹)
- A circle for Bob (S¹)

The joint space is therefore:

S¹ × S¹  =  a **torus**

This torus is not a physical donut in 3D space.  
It is the **configuration space of the pair’s internal orientations**.

It plays the same role as phase space in classical mechanics:  
a geometric arena in which the state of the system lives.

---

## 2. What Lives on the Torus

For a product (unentangled) state, the joint amplitude factorizes:

Ψ(θ_A, θ_B) = ψ_A(θ_A) ψ_B(θ_B)

Geometrically:  
The torus carries a separable texture — stripes parallel to the axes.

For an entangled state (e.g. the singlet), the joint amplitude is:

Ψ(θ_A, θ_B) ∝ cos((θ_A − θ_B)/2)

This is **not separable**.  
It depends only on the relative angle, and forms a **slanted helical pattern** wrapping around the torus.

This slanted pattern is the “double helix”:
two counter-winding spinor strands (σ = ±1) whose interference produces the cosine.

So the torus carries:

- Bright/dark bands:  |Ψ(θ_A, θ_B)|²  (Born weight)
- A twisted phase field:  S(θ_A, θ_B)  (spinor phase)

---

## 3. Measurement as Slicing the Torus

Each analyzer setting defines regions on the torus:

- Alice’s outcome ± corresponds to **vertical bands** in θ_A.
- Bob’s outcome ± corresponds to **horizontal bands** in θ_B.

Thus the torus is partitioned into four kinds of regions:

(+A,+B), (+A,−B), (−A,+B), (−A,−B)

The correlations come from how the **helical texture intersects these bands**.

---

## 4. Why No-Signaling Is Automatic

Bob’s local statistics are obtained by ignoring Alice’s result:

P_B(±) = ∫ dθ_A |Ψ(θ_A, θ_B)|²

Geometrically:
this is integrating the torus texture **along the θ_A direction**.

Because the helix winds symmetrically, its total weight in Bob’s “+” band
equals its weight in Bob’s “−” band, for any analyzer choice.

So:

- Correlations change with relative angle.
- Marginals stay 50/50.

This is why the geometry is nonlocal but not a signaling channel.

---

## 5. The Torus and the Double Helix

Your “double helix” language maps precisely:

- The two helicities σ = ±1 are the two spinor windings.
- Their phase difference creates a standing interference pattern.
- That pattern lives on the joint-angle torus, not in 3D space.

The pair is a **single geometric object** whose natural coordinates are
(θ_A, θ_B), not two independent local states.

---

## 6. Bohmian / Pilot-Wave Interpretation

In Bohmian language, the torus carries a phase field:

Ψ(θ_A, θ_B) = R(θ_A, θ_B) e^{i S(θ_A, θ_B)}

Local rotor dynamics are guided by the phase gradient:

dθ_A/dt ∝ ∂S/∂θ_A  
dθ_B/dt ∝ ∂S/∂θ_B  

So:

- The “pilot wave” is the helical phase structure on the torus.
- The particles’ local orientations are guided by a **global geometry**.
- No collapse is needed; only motion on the joint phase landscape.

---

## 7. Why This Demystifies Entanglement

Entanglement is not:

- Superluminal messaging
- Prewritten lookup tables
- Disembodied probability magic

It is:

> A continuous spinor geometry on the torus of joint angles,
> probed locally by square-law detectors.

The mystery dissolves when you stop asking:
“what does each particle carry?”

and start asking:
“what geometry does the pair as a whole inhabit?”

The answer is:  
**a twisted, double-helical structure on S¹ × S¹.**
