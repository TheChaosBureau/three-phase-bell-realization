## 1. What the “double helix” really is

When we write the joint amplitude

[  
\mathcal{A}_{ab} = \tfrac12\sum_{\sigma=\pm1} e^{i\sigma(\phi_a-\phi_b)/2},  
]

Geometrically:

- In single-particle language, a spinor has two components (σ = ±1).
    
- In the singlet, you don’t assign one to A and the other to B. Both helicities belong to the pair, as a whole.
    
- The “double helix” is just that: the pair’s **two-component structure**, which is probed when you ask about both analyzers together.
