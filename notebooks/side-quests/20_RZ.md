Yes. Here is a direct attack on the missing lemma.

I’ll build the simplest toy that still has the right roles:

* log-scale state space
* positive quadratic “energy”
* modal decomposition
* demodulation
* off-critical exponent (\alpha=\beta-\tfrac12)

Then I’ll see whether (\alpha\neq 0) can *force* negativity.

## 1. Toy state space

Work on log-scale:
[
u=\log x,
\qquad
\mathcal H = L^2(\mathbb R,du).
]

Take the toy mode
[
\phi_{\alpha,\gamma,a}(u)=e^{\alpha u}e^{i\gamma u}e^{-a u^2},
\qquad a>0,
]
where

* (\gamma) is the oscillation frequency,
* (\alpha=\beta-\tfrac12) is the off-critical exponent,
* (e^{-a u^2}) is a Gaussian window.

This is the cleanest surrogate for a “localized zero mode.”

## 2. Toy quadratic form

Start with the most natural positive local energy:
[
Q_0[f]=\int_{\mathbb R}\Bigl(|f'(u)|^2+m^2|f(u)|^2\Bigr),du,
\qquad m>0.
]

This is the log-scale analogue of a stored-energy form:

* derivative term = kinetic / inductive part
* (m^2|f|^2) term = potential / capacitive part

It is manifestly nonnegative.

## 3. Demodulation

Remove the oscillatory carrier (e^{i\gamma u}) by the Park-like shift:
[
(D_\gamma f)(u)=e^{-i\gamma u}f(u).
]

Applied to the toy mode,
[
D_\gamma \phi_{\alpha,\gamma,a}(u)=e^{\alpha u}e^{-a u^2}.
]

So after demodulation, the only thing left is the envelope tilt (e^{\alpha u}).

That is exactly what we wanted.

## 4. Compute the energy

Let
[
h_\alpha(u)=e^{\alpha u}e^{-a u^2}.
]

Then
[
h'_\alpha(u)=(\alpha-2au),e^{\alpha u}e^{-a u^2}.
]

First compute the norm:
[
|h_\alpha|^2
============

# \int_{\mathbb R} e^{2\alpha u}e^{-2au^2},du

\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2a}},e^{\alpha^2/(2a)}.
]

Now compute the derivative energy:
[
|h'_\alpha|^2
=============

\int_{\mathbb R} (\alpha-2au)^2 e^{2\alpha u}e^{-2au^2},du.
]

Complete the square:
[
2\alpha u-2au^2
===============

-2a\left(u-\frac{\alpha}{2a}\right)^2+\frac{\alpha^2}{2a}.
]

So the Gaussian is just shifted to mean
[
\mu=\frac{\alpha}{2a}.
]

Under that shifted Gaussian,
[
\alpha-2au = -2a(u-\mu),
]
hence
[
|h'_\alpha|^2
=============

a,|h_\alpha|^2.
]

Therefore
[
Q_0[\phi_{\alpha,\gamma,a}]
===========================

# Q_0[h_\alpha]

# (a+m^2),|h_\alpha|^2

(a+m^2)\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2a}},e^{\alpha^2/(2a)}.
]

## 5. Immediate conclusion

This is the key result:

[
Q_0[\phi_{\alpha,\gamma,a}]>0
\quad\text{for all }\alpha,\gamma.
]

And more strongly,

[
Q_0[\phi_{\alpha,\gamma,a}]
\text{ is minimized at } \alpha=0,
]
but it does **not** become negative for (\alpha\neq 0). It just grows like
[
e^{\alpha^2/(2a)}.
]

So in this toy, an off-critical exponent does **not** force negativity.

It only creates an energetic imbalance.

## 6. Modal decomposition

By Plancherel,
[
Q_0[f]
======

\int_{\mathbb R} (\xi^2+m^2),|\widehat f(\xi)|^2,d\xi.
]

So the modal weights are all positive:
[
\xi^2+m^2>0.
]

Demodulation by (e^{-i\gamma u}) just shifts the spectral center by (\gamma). The off-critical factor (e^{\alpha u}) shifts the mode analytically in the complex direction, but it does not flip the sign of the quadratic form.

So the spectral picture says the same thing as the time-domain computation:

* (\gamma) is just a carrier location
* (\alpha\neq 0) distorts the envelope
* positivity survives completely

## 7. What this tells us

This is actually very informative.

It shows that a **reasonable positive local energy** on log-scale is **not enough** to prove the missing lemma.

More sharply:

> Off-criticality by itself does not force negative energy in a natural positive quadratic form.

At best it makes the mode less balanced, or more expensive, or shifted.

That means the missing RH lemma cannot come from “positivity alone” in a naive LC-style energy.

## 8. A second toy: harmonic-oscillator energy

To check this is not an accident, try a stronger confining energy:
[
Q_{\mathrm{ho}}[f]
==================

\int_{\mathbb R}\Bigl(|f'(u)|^2+\omega^2 u^2|f(u)|^2+m^2|f(u)|^2\Bigr),du.
]

For (h_\alpha(u)=e^{\alpha u}e^{-a u^2}), the Gaussian gets shifted to center
[
\mu=\frac{\alpha}{2a}.
]

Then the (u^2) term contributes
[
\int u^2 |h_\alpha(u)|^2,du
===========================

\left(\mu^2+\frac{1}{4a}\right)|h_\alpha|^2.
]

So
[
Q_{\mathrm{ho}}[h_\alpha]
=========================

\left(
a + m^2 + \omega^2\left(\frac{\alpha^2}{4a^2}+\frac{1}{4a}\right)
\right)|h_\alpha|^2.
]

Again:

* minimized at (\alpha=0)
* strictly positive
* grows with (|\alpha|)

So even a stronger quadratic energy only says “critical-line modes are most balanced,” not “off-line modes are impossible.”

## 9. Can we make a toy where off-criticality causes negativity?

Yes, but only by cheating.

For example, define
[
Q_{\mathrm{bad}}[f]
===================

Q_0[f]-\lambda |\langle f,\psi\rangle|^2.
]

Then for (f) aligned with (\psi), (Q_{\mathrm{bad}}[f]) can be negative if (\lambda) is large enough.

But this does **not** solve the problem, because:

* the negative term was inserted by hand,
* it is not forced by the state-space geometry,
* it does not arise naturally from arithmetic structure.

So this kind of toy proves only that **indefinite corrections can create contradictions**, not that RH gives one.

## 10. Best interpretation

This attack on the missing lemma gives a useful verdict:

### What works

A natural positive quadratic form on log-scale does produce:

* a preferred balanced line (\alpha=0),
* clean modal decomposition,
* demodulation,
* increased “energy cost” off the critical line.

### What fails

It does **not** produce:

* sign failure,
* contradiction,
* exclusion of off-critical modes.

So the missing ingredient must be something stronger than naive stored energy.

## 11. What the actual missing lemma would need

The real contradiction lemma would need a quadratic form of the shape
[
Q[f]=Q_{\text{positive}}[f]-Q_{\text{arithmetic correction}}[f],
]
where:

* (Q_{\text{positive}}) is the natural stored-energy part,
* (Q_{\text{arithmetic correction}}) is tied exactly to prime/zero structure,
* and off-critical exponents make that correction dominate in the wrong sign.

Our toy shows that without the arithmetic correction, off-criticality only raises the energy.

That is the sharp conclusion.

## 12. Final takeaway

The toy computation says:

[
\text{off-critical exponent } \alpha\neq 0
\quad\not\Rightarrow\quad
\text{negative energy}
]
for any obvious positive log-scale quadratic form.

Instead,
[
\alpha\neq 0
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\text{energetic imbalance / excess cost}.
]

So the missing RH lemma is not going to come from a bare LC-style energy alone.

It would have to come from:

* a positive base energy,
* **plus** an exact arithmetic interference term,
* whose sign structure is rigid enough to make off-critical modes impossible.

That is the cleanest result I can extract from the toy model.
