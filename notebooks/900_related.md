### 15.6 Which kind of nonlocality is being claimed

The program is explicitly nonlocal, but "nonlocal" is ambiguous across at least three distinct senses that are not interchangeable, and the preferred chain is only committed to one of them:

1. **Configurational nonlocality.** The shared state lives in a joint state space (here the four-dimensional shared core of §10.2). Dynamics are local in that joint space but do not factor into independent 3-space-local pieces. This is the Bohm-style sense, and it is the sense the preferred chain does commit to.
2. **Signaling nonlocality.** Operational no-signaling is violated: a setting change at one analyzer is detectable in the marginal statistics at the other. The preferred chain is **not** committed to this. The front-end has no pre-click feedback, the detector boundary is local per branch, the latch is local, and the closure/drain only activates after a winner is captured. Pre-click transparency (§12.2) is the concrete evidence that no-signaling holds at the reduced level.
3. **Correlational nonlocality.** No-signaling is preserved, but joint statistics exhibit correlations that cannot be reproduced by any local common-cause model — the sense in which standard quantum mechanics is nonlocal via Bell.

The preferred chain clearly exhibits (1) and clearly avoids (2). Whether it exhibits (3) in the strict Bell sense, or only appears to because the shared four-dimensional core was constructed to carry the target joint weights, is not settled by the current validation. The shared core could be interpreted either as a genuine joint configuration with irreducible configurational nonlocality, or as a correlational register that happens to be implemented as a 4-state object. These are indistinguishable at the level of the current metrics but differ in what they claim about the underlying ontology. Future writeups should pick one reading explicitly and not let the distinction slide.

### 15A. Relation to existing programs

The preferred chain is not an interpretation of quantum mechanics in the usual philosophical sense, and it is not a hidden-variable theory in the Bell sense. Structurally, it sits closest to two existing research programs, and readers familiar with either will recognize components of the chain immediately.

**Prequantum Classical Statistical Field Theory (PCSFT).** Khrennikov's PCSFT program treats the quantum state as a classical random field and derives Born-like detection statistics from the combination of (a) squared projections of the field onto analyzer modes and (b) threshold / nucleation events in detectors rather than accumulation. The single-delta linear energy-weight law of §3 is structurally the PCSFT projection move, and the shot-trigger / rare-event detector family of §5–6 is structurally the PCSFT threshold-detection move. The program's narrowing of the detector mechanism space (accumulator detectors fail, rare first-event detectors succeed) is the same phenomenon PCSFT relies on, arrived at here independently from engineering constraints rather than from a prequantum field axiomatics. Key references: Khrennikov, *Detection Model Based on Representation of Quantum Particles by Classical Random Fields: Born's Rule and Beyond*, Foundations of Physics **39**, 997–1022 (2009); Khrennikov, *Prequantum Classical Statistical Field Theory: Simulation of Probabilities of Photon Detection with the Aid of Classical Brownian Motion*, arXiv:1412.8036 (2014).

**The Allahverdyan–Balian–Nieuwenhuizen (ABN) Curie–Weiss measurement model.** The ABN program solves a Hamiltonian model of measurement in which an apparatus prepared in a metastable state is driven into one of several stable pointer states by interaction with the measured system, with Born-rule statistics emerging from the apparatus dynamics rather than from a collapse postulate. The winner-latch and post-click closure/drain layers of the present chain (§7, §11) play the same architectural role as the metastable-to-stable phase transition in ABN: a physically intelligible apparatus-side process that converts continuous pre-click branch weights into a single committed outcome, with no pre-click feedback to the state. The separation enforced here between "front-end computes branch weights" and "apparatus commits to one outcome" is the same separation ABN enforces between truncation and registration. Key references: Allahverdyan, Balian & Nieuwenhuizen, *Curie–Weiss model of the quantum measurement process*, Europhys. Lett. **61**, 452–458 (2003); Allahverdyan, Balian & Nieuwenhuizen, *Understanding quantum measurement from the solution of dynamical models*, Physics Reports **525**, 1–166 (2013) — the definitive review, often called the "Opus."

**How the present program differs.** Neither PCSFT nor ABN uses strict closed-system energy accounting as a credibility constraint on the measurement chain, and neither frames the front-end as a resonant shared-mode power-system network. The combination of (a) explicit squared-projection energy-weight law at the front-end, (b) rare-event detector selection, (c) metastable winner latch, (d) post-click closure/drain, and (e) whole-trial energy balance closing to machine precision is specific to this program. The relationship to PCSFT and ABN should be read as convergent structure, not derivation from either.

The clearest parallel, and I think it's quite close, is the **objective collapse / dynamical reduction family** — GRW, CSL, Diósi–Penrose. Look at what your architecture is actually doing: you have a real, shared, wavefunction-like ontology (the resonant modal state), unitary-like pre-click evolution that leaves branch amplitudes untouched, and then a *rare stochastic nucleation event* that picks one branch, followed by a physical suppression of the others. That is structurally the GRW story almost line for line. GRW's "hits" are exactly your `shot_trigger` rare-event nucleation; GRW's post-hit localization — the mechanism that makes the other branches stop contributing — is exactly your winner-gated shunt drain plus common inhibit rail. Even the insistence on pre-click transparency (no feedback onto the branch weights before a hit) mirrors how CSL is engineered so the stochastic term doesn't distort ordinary dynamics until a localization event fires.

There's a secondary resonance with **Bohmian / pilot-wave** thinking, but it's weaker and it's located in a different place. The Bohmian flavor is in your commitment to a real, shared, nonlocal state that is *doing physical work* rather than being bookkeeping — the modal state isn't an agent's information, it's ontic, and the nonlocality is baked into the geometry of the shared resonator rather than sneaked in through a measurement postulate. But you don't have the Bohmian move of positing a separate particle ontology riding on top of the wave, so the pilot-wave parallel is more about metaphysical posture (ψ-ontic, overtly nonlocal, mechanism-first) than about formal structure.

What's genuinely distinctive about your program, and I don't think it maps cleanly onto any standard interpretation, is that you've taken the **Heisenberg cut and made it into an engineered boundary with a contract**. In standard QM the cut is notoriously movable and mysterious — von Neumann showed it can slide anywhere from the apparatus to the observer's retina without changing predictions, which is part of why measurement feels like a black box inside a black box. Your architecture refuses that sliding: the state/measurement split is frozen at a specific interface (the calibrated detector boundary), the front-end is explicitly the "unitary" side, the detector+latch+drain is explicitly the "collapse" side, and the handoff between them is a calibrated engineering contract rather than a postulate. That's closer in spirit to how people like Allahverdyan, Balian, and Nieuwenhuizen try to derive measurement from explicit apparatus dynamics — the "measurement as a physical process in a concrete apparatus model" tradition — than it is to any of the textbook interpretations.

The honest caveats, which your freeze note already states but are worth naming in this framing: (1) GRW/CSL are empirically distinguishable from standard QM in principle, and a real hardware realization of your chain would face the same question — does it deviate from QM somewhere, and if so where? (2) Being ψ-ontic and explicitly nonlocal puts you safely on the "gave up locality" side of Bell's forced choice, so you don't owe anyone a locality story, but it also means the program inherits the standard discomforts of nonlocal realism (preferred frame issues, relativistic compatibility). (3) Reproducing CHSH at the correlator level in a shared-state analog is not the same as reproducing the full tensor-product structure, and the gap between "good CHSH fidelity in a reduced model" and "quantum-complete joint system" is exactly where classical analogs historically get into trouble. You clearly know all three of these, which is why the freeze note is careful about what it does and doesn't claim — but in the taxonomy question you asked, I'd file the program as **"objective collapse, engineered-apparatus flavor, with a Bohmian-adjacent ontic commitment to a shared nonlocal state."** That's an unusual combination and I think it's the most accurate one-line placement.
